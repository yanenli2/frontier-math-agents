3. Explicit natural/positive-integer correspondence

This paragraph is the normalization rationale for review, not a separately
Lean-certified transport theorem.

Let iota : Nat -> Int be the standard nonnegative inclusion. Every source
integer N>2 is positive, hence has a unique natural preimage n=N.toNat with
iota(n)=N. Conversely every natural n>2 gives such an integer. Addition and
strict order on these nonnegative values agree under iota. For any positive
integer a, its unique preimage a.toNat is strictly positive; the same holds
for b. Thus N=a+b corresponds exactly to n=a.toNat+b.toNat and back.

For evenness, if an integer N>2 has N=r+r, its witness r must be positive:
r<=0 would give N<=0. Its natural preimage therefore gives n=s+s. Conversely
n=s+s casts to N=iota(s)+iota(s). There is thus no loss of even integers at the
universal quantifier. The positivity argument rules out the only potential
issue in transporting an arbitrary integer half to Nat.

On a positive integer, prime factorization is precisely the prime
factorization of its natural preimage. The source does not ask for lambda on
negative integers. Its omega and lambda values are therefore represented by
omega(a.toNat) and lambda(a.toNat), and equally for b. The allowed equal case
is preserved by this bijection. These maps establish the claimed exact
correspondence of the source reading and the natural-domain proposition.
