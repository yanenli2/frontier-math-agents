4. DEPENDENCY-ORDERED PROOF PLAN AFTER REVIEW

Wave A: small seeds and direct specializations.
- F01 from lambda_mul at 2,3 and the accepted prime values.
- F02 from lambda_prime and pinned Nat.prime_seven.
- F03 uses independent witnesses (5,7) and (6,6), with exact sums 12.
- F04 applies the accepted representation_two_sign_seed at d=12.
- F05 uses lambda_two_mul on m and the diagonal witnesses (2m,2m), or the
  accepted representation_diagonal after proving lambda(4m)=1.
- F06 writes m=k+k, derives k>0, and specializes all-8k.
- F07 writes m=3t, derives t>0, and normalizes 4m=12t before F04.

Wave B: the two-squares construction, independent of Fermat.
- Prove F08 with exactly the zero/equality cases in A:111–123.
- If u=v, hSum and m>0 imply u²>0 and 4m=8u²; use all-8t with t=u².
- Otherwise order the coordinates. Write the larger as the smaller plus w,
  where w>0. This avoids relying on truncated natural subtraction for |u-v|.
  The two roots are u+v and w; both are positive. Their doubled squares have
  lambda=-1 by lambda_square followed by lambda_two_mul. The sum identity is
  a polynomial identity after substituting larger=smaller+w, hence kernel ring
  arithmetic suffices. The resulting witnesses are 2(u+v)² and 2|u-v|².
- When one coordinate is zero these witnesses can coincide; this is allowed.
  Never use the unequal formula when u=v, since it would introduce a zero
  second witness. No stronger positivity condition may be added to F08.

Wave C: the prime-1-mod-4 input.
- Prove F09 using the exact pinned library theorem documented in §5, after the
  narrow required module is available and its type/axioms actually checked.
  This is an authorized alternate derivation of the same two-squares statement;
  A:125–151 supplies the separately reviewed elementary proof if needed.
- F10 combines F09, prime positivity, and F08. The proposed interface does not
  need nonzero or unequal square coordinates because F08 already handles them.

Wave D: extraction and sign-preserving scaling.
- F11 obtains a natural cofactor from p∣m, proves it positive from m>0, rewrites
  lambda m with lambda_mul, and uses lambda p=-1 to force lambda d=1.
  Normalize m=p*d to m=d*p by commutativity; do not divide any representation.
- F12 uses m≠1 (lambda_one and hSign) plus Nat.exists_prime_and_dvd, then F11;
  alternatively take the head of the nonempty primeFactorsList as in the
  earlier failed exact-target attempt. In either route d=1 remains allowed.
- F13 applies F11 and accepted hasSignedRepresentation_mul to hCore, with
  lambda d=1, then normalizes d*(4p)=4m.
- F14 splits lambda m: F05 in the positive case, F10 then F13 in the negative
  case. No assumption that m is odd is required.
- F18: forward specialize m=p using hp.pos. In reverse, first use F05/F06;
  otherwise use F12. From m=d*p and ¬Even m prove p≠2, apply the stated odd-prime
  core, and scale by d. This is a closed equivalence, not a proof of the core.

Wave E: all m not congruent to 3 mod 4.
- F15 is the only genuinely new multiplicity/residue bookkeeping obligation.
  From m%4=1 obtain oddness of m. Suppose no prime divisor has residue 1.
  Every prime occurrence in m.primeFactorsList divides m, cannot be 2, and has
  residue 1 or 3 by primality/oddness; hence every occurrence has residue 3.
- Preserve repetitions: use the actual prime-factor LIST and its length omega m,
  not the distinct-prime finset. Its product is m since hm gives m≠0.
- Lambda's negative sign gives Odd (omega m) from the pinned negative-one-power
  parity lemma. By list induction, a list whose entries are all 3 modulo 4 has
  product congruent to 3^length modulo 4. An odd length makes that remainder 3
  (3²≡1 modulo 4), contradicting m%4=1. This proves the required prime exists.
  List.prod_nat_mod is available in pinned source as an optional convenience;
  direct list induction with Nat.mul_mod avoids any extra import if preferred.
- F16 splits lambda m. The positive case is F05; the negative case uses F15 to
  select p and F14 for the representation. The case m=1 is therefore covered.
- F17 splits Even m. That case is F06. Otherwise m is odd; a natural odd
  remainder modulo 4 is 1 or 3, and hMod excludes 3. Apply F16. Thus residue
  classes 0,1,2 are uniformly covered, without asserting failure at class 3.

Wave F: sharper exact residual equivalence.
- F19 can reuse F18 rather than repeat factor extraction. An odd prime p is
  either 1 or 3 modulo 4. The first case is F10. For residue 3, split p=3:
  use F04 at t=1 there; otherwise primality/residue bounds give 7≤p and the
  stated C3 premise applies. The forward direction is specialization to m=p.
- The final theorem representation_multiple_four is still absent. Do not add
  a sorry, axiom, hC3 parameter, finite computation, or alleged positivity
  consequence of the counting identity to manufacture it.
