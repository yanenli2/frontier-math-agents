Cyclic construction, matching source §4.2 / blueprint §6:
- Install Fact p.Prime locally from the explicit hp. Set r(i)=((i:ZMod p)*z).val
  and b(i)=n*r(i)/p. Prove r(i)<p and b(i)<n.
- On indices below n<p, equal representatives imply equal residue multiples;
  cancel the nonzero z using the prime-modulus field structure, then compare
  canonical values below p. Thus distinct indices have distinct representatives.
- If some b(i)=n-1, take k=-(i:Int) and d=p-r(i). The final-bin condition forces
  i≠0. The lower floor bound gives n*d≤p. Equality would imply n divides the
  prime p, contradicted by 2≤n<p. Thus n*d<p, strictly. The residue equality
  handles the wrapping gap by casting the bounded natural difference p-r(i).
- Otherwise b maps range n into range(n-1). The exact finite-cardinality
  pigeonhole API produces distinct equal-bin indices. Order their values and
  take d=r(j)-r(i), k=(j:Int)-(i:Int). The common-bin lower and strict upper
  bounds give n*d<p. The two indices in [0,n) give k≠0 and k.natAbs<n; this is
  proved through integer absolute-value inequalities, not a non-strict bound.
- The residue equation follows by casting the bounded representative difference.
  Signed k is essential: the value ordering need not equal the index ordering.

No lambda, nonrepresentation, antireflection, character law, or final endpoint

Ternary construction, matching source §§1–3 / blueprint §5:
- A1: if lambda(p-n) were negative too, the positive witnesses4n and4(p-n)
  would contradict hno. The multiplier4 has positive sign.
- A2: if lambda(2p-n) were positive too, the doubled positive-sign arguments
  would produce two negative witnesses of total4p. Both natural differences
  are proved strictly positive before using lambda's positive-input facts.
- A3: a positive-sign member x=3u would force lambda u=-1. A1 then makes
  lambda(p-u)=1, hence lambda(3(p-u))=-1. A2 at y supplies another negative
  argument2p-y, and these positive arguments sum to4p, contradicting hno.
- A4: apply A3 to both entries. Primality and p≠3 exclude divisibility of p
  by3. Explicitly split the two possible nonzero residues of x and y modulo3;
  opposite residues contradict p's nonzero remainder. This proves exact
  divisibility of p+x and p+y by3. Let u=(p+x)/3 and v=(p+y)/3. The exact
  equations3u=p+x and3v=p+y establish positive ordered members, totalp and a
  smaller natural gap. A2 and lambda_three give both new signs+1.
- A5: use strong induction on the positive-pair gap, with both entries quantified
  in the induction predicate. A4 supplies the strictly smaller gap. For an
  arbitrary pair, swap entries if needed; equality of entries is impossible
  because hp and p≠2 give oddness of p. This rules out ALL positive-positive
  pairs of totalp, not merely a chosen ordered pair.
- A6: split lambda n. Its negative branch is A1; its positive branch uses A5
  and sign dichotomy to force the negative complement. The resulting
  antireflection conclusion holds on every0<n<p, not just the local third-range.
